# Escape Perfectionism

A first-person browser escape room where you experience what it feels like to have a perfectionist mind — and learn how to get through it.

## The Unique Kernel

Most games reward finishing perfectly. This one rewards starting at all. You play through the paralysis, fear, and intrusive thoughts of perfectionism — not to "fix" yourself, but to understand what's happening and walk away with real tools for your daily life. The win isn't a perfect task. The win is taking the action at all and being less hard on yourself.

## Who It's For

Someone who has stared at a task they need to do — a project, an assignment, a creative thing — and just... couldn't start. They know they're procrastinating but can't stop. They've felt that chest-tightening spiral. They want to understand why, or they want to feel less alone in it. They don't necessarily identify as a "gamer."

## The Core Loop

You're looking at your desk. The task list in front of you is already impossible — "finish 2 chapters in an hour," "tackle 3 projects today." You try to start. The cursor hesitates. Clicking around the scene lets you explore — you open a drawer and find a journal inside. You take it out; it lands on the desk. Then a thought challenge hits: fear, the urge to check your phone, a headache creeping in, feeling crushed by how much you have to do. You choose how to respond. One of the choices is "go to your journal" — it's right there on the desk now, where you put it. Opening it is how you uncover what the feeling actually is, decoded in your own words. Some choices push you forward; some spiral you deeper. The screen shifts with your mental state — darker and more anxious when thoughts overwhelm, subtly warmer as you push through. When the task cycle ends, you don't feel triumphant. The game names what just happened honestly, then gives you a real insight. The journal was never a menu or a UI element — it was something in a drawer you found yourself.

## Inspiration & Identity

- **Dark Domes escape rooms** — atmospheric, detailed, slightly unsettling art; the feeling of being inside a real environment you have to read carefully (https://darkdome.com)
- **First-person POV games** — you inhabit the character, not observe them
- **Inner-voice fiction** — books and films that put you inside the character's head, naming feelings, tracing causes and effects
- **Mood:** Quiet. A little eerie. Emotionally honest. Not horror — more like a rainy afternoon that feels heavier than it should.
- **Color:** Muted, cool tones that shift warmer as you move through. Anxiety = gloomy and desaturated. Progress = subtle warmth.

## Why This Matters to the Learner

This is personal. The learner lives with severe perfectionism — it shapes how they see themselves, their relationships, their work, their body, their day. "It could be an illness." They want to take something heavy and make it into something that could help other people. Building it is itself an act of processing it.

> "i want the player to go through having a perfectionist mind and face it... at the end not to be hard on their selves"

## What "Working" Looks Like

You open the game in a browser. You see a desk — journal on the left, a laptop or monitor overwhelmed with tabs in front of you. You try to click on a task. The cursor hesitates. An intrusive thought appears. You choose a response. The environment reacts. You fight through two or three thought challenges. You complete the task — and the game reflects back that disappointed-but-relieved feeling accurately, then gives you an insight: *"You finished it. That's the part a perfectionist never celebrates. Action is the real win."* The screen settles. A closing message: be less hard on yourself.

That's the demo moment. That's what goes in the video.

## The POC Boundary

**In:**
- One desk scene, fully drawn — desk with drawer, laptop/monitor drowning in tabs, atmospheric lighting
- Task list with visibly impossible/unrealistic goals already written on it
- One task cycle: start → intrusive thought challenges (2–3) → completion → insight + closing message
- Thought challenges: fear of starting, urge to check phone, physical symptoms (head hurts), comparison, unrealistic goal disappointment, feeling overwhelmed — each appears as text + choice
- Journal discovery: player opens a desk drawer while exploring → finds the journal → it moves onto the desk
- "Go to your journal" as a recurring choice during challenges → opens journal → decodes the feeling
- Visual state system — screen desaturates and darkens with anxiety, clears with progress
- A closing self-compassion screen with real insight about perfectionism

**Not needed to prove the idea:**
- Multiple tasks or levels
- A full game arc across many scenes
- Sound design (desirable but not required to prove the concept)
- Mobile layout
- A backend or save system

## Later

- Multiple task cycles with different perfectionism patterns (imposter syndrome task, comparison spiral task, etc.)
- Sound design: ambient noise, inner monologue as audio
- An unlockable "journal decoded" screen showing the full emotional map
- Multiple endings depending on choices made
- Mobile-responsive layout

## Explicitly Cut

- **Constant comparison** and **talking more than acting** — included as thought challenge types, not cut; listed here previously in error
- **A "perfect run" reward or high score** — cut because it would contradict the entire point of the game
- **Multiplayer or social features** — not relevant to the solo inner-experience concept
- **A backend/database** — nothing needs to be saved or tracked server-side for the PoC; adds complexity with no proof-of-concept value
- **Full narrated voiceover** — meaningful but out of scope for a 2–4 hour build; can be added later
