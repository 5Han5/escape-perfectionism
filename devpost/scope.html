Escape Perfectionism

A first-person browser escape room where you experience what it feels like to have a perfectionist mind — and learn how to get through it.

Status: Draft — awaiting approval
The Unique Kernel

The win isn't finishing perfectly. It's starting at all.

Most games reward perfection. This one rewards action. You play through the paralysis, fear, and intrusive thoughts of perfectionism — not to "fix" yourself, but to understand what's happening and walk away with real tools for your daily life.

"Action is the real victory that a perfectionist never celebrates."

The Journal Mechanic

Discovered through play, not through menus

The journal is an object in the room — not a UI element. It's found by exploring, used when it matters most.

"Check your journal" — one of the choices
Opening the journal during a challenge reveals what the feeling actually is — decoded in the character's own words. Not a tooltip. Not a label. A moment of self-awareness.
Thought Challenges

Perfectionism patterns the player has to face

Each appears as text + a set of choices. One choice is always "check your journal." The environment reacts to what you pick.

🔒 Fear of Starting
If I start, I might fail — so I won't start at all.
📱 Urge to Check Phone
Anything to avoid sitting with the discomfort.
🤕 Physical Symptoms
Head hurts. Body says stop before the mind does.
🔄 Constant Comparison
"Someone else would have done this better already."
💬 Talk Over Action
Planning and preparing endlessly instead of doing.
📉 Unrealistic Goals
2 chapters in an hour. 3 projects today. Already set up to fail.
Who It's For

Someone staring at a task they can't start

They know they're procrastinating. They can't stop. They've felt that chest-tightening spiral. They want to understand why — or just feel less alone in it. They don't need to be a gamer.

Why This Matters

This is personal

The learner lives with severe perfectionism. It shapes how they see themselves, their relationships, their work, their body, their day. Building this is itself an act of processing it — and making something that could help others carry what they carry.

"I want the player to go through having a perfectionist mind and face it... at the end, not to be hard on their selves."
What "Working" Looks Like

The demo moment

You open the game in a browser. A desk — journal on the left, laptop drowning in tabs, a task list with goals that are already impossible. You click around. "Explore surroundings?" — you find the journal. You try to start a task. A thought hits. You choose to check the journal. It decodes the feeling. You push through two or three challenges. You finish.

The game reflects the honest ending: relieved, but not satisfied. Maybe still blaming yourself.

"You finished it.
That's the part a perfectionist never celebrates.
Action is the real win.

Be less hard on yourself."
Proof of Concept Boundary

What's in, what's later, what's cut

✓ In — PoC
  • One desk scene — journal, tabs, atmospheric lighting
  • Task list with impossible goals already written
  • "Explore surroundings?" journal discovery
  • 2–3 thought challenges (text + choices)
  • "Check journal" as a recurring choice
  • Visual anxiety state system
  • Insight + self-compassion ending
  • Runs in browser, no install
→ Later
  • Multiple task cycles / levels
  • Inner monologue audio
  • Journal decoded full screen
  • Multiple endings by choices
  • Mobile layout
  • Narrated voiceover
✕ Cut — with reason
  • Perfect run reward / high score — contradicts the entire point
  • Multiplayer or social — solo inner experience only
  • Backend / database — nothing needs saving for PoC
Inspiration & Identity

Atmospheric. Emotionally honest. Quiet and eerie.

Dark Dome escape rooms — visual reference
Detailed environments, atmospheric lighting, slightly unsettling quality. The feeling of being inside a real place you have to read carefully. darkdome.com
First-person POV games & films
You inhabit the character, not observe them. The camera is the self. What you see is filtered through the character's mental state.
Inner-voice fiction
Books and films that put you inside the character's head — naming feelings, tracing causes and effects, giving texture to the sounds of anxious thought.
Color & mood
Muted, cool tones that shift warmer as you move through. Anxiety = gloomy and desaturated. Progress = subtle warmth bleeding back in. The ending is relieved, not triumphant.
